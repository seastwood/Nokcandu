# Nokcandu 0.12.18

## Controller profiles

### Gyro
- **Turn about gravity:** left and right follow turning the controller about the vertical, however it's tipped or rolled in your hands. In the gyro's "Left and right comes from" section.
- **Real-world sensitivity:** set how far the view turns per degree you turn the controller (1 × is one to one), from the game's mouse movement for a full turn.
- **Gyro can scroll:** tip the controller back or forward to scroll, with its own speed.

### Original Steam Controller
- **A trackpad can be an analog stick.** The left pad works as the left stick next to the joystick (whichever is pushed further counts), with its own dead zone and angle, so you can walk slowly or run in controller games. The right pad can be a right stick with the same settings.
- **Tap a trackpad** — a quick touch and lift — to press a key or button, and **double-tap** for something else. Works in every pad behavior.
- **Divide a trackpad into zones** — halves, four corners, or centre and four sides. A tap, double tap or click in each zone can do something of its own, whatever the pad is doing (trackball, mouse, stick, directions…). A zone with nothing set uses the pad's ordinary tap or click. The zones turn with the pad angle and show on the controller picture, lighting up as you use them.
- **The Steam Controller rumbles itself.** Test Rumble, "Rumble when the layer changes" and a button set to "Rumble" now buzz the Steam Controller's trackpads when it's the profile's controller. Before, they went to whatever other controller macOS could rumble, such as a Switch Pro Controller. The light settings, which it doesn't have, are hidden for it.
- A button that switches layers shows the layer's name on the picture instead of its first four letters.
- **Set the layer-change rumble's strength and length**, and try it from the profile's Feedback section. It is gentler by default.
- **A trackpad can be a scroll wheel:** circle your thumb around the pad to scroll, the way the Steam Controller was designed. Choose how far round each step is, reverse it, and feel a haptic tick for every step with its own strength.
- **Radial menus picked with the mouse or gyro work properly.** Movement steers a small virtual stick, so small wobbles no longer flick between choices, and the gyro picks even when it's normally on only while a pad is touched.

## Fixes
- **Steadier gyro calibration.** Automatic calibration ran almost constantly in the hand and could mistake slow, steady aiming for drift, which felt like the gyro slowing down. It now waits for a second and a half of real stillness and measures at most every 30 seconds, and a wrong measurement is corrected the next time the controller rests.
- **Scrolling no longer moves the cursor** in older games (Battlefield 1942, Battlefield 2 and others) while raw mouse capture or a controller is moving it.
- **DirectX 8–11 games through DXVK are no longer forced to render one frame at a time,** which made the processor and graphics chip wait for each other. They now work as DXVK intends, which can raise the frame rate; Performance → Low-latency Aiming still gives you one frame when you want it.
- **The right trackpad no longer stalls mid-swipe or hooks sideways.** "Small movement steadiness" (an older setting) and the newer Smoothing were both holding back small movements, so the cursor paused and one direction froze then jumped. Smoothing now replaces steadiness; steadiness only applies when Smoothing is off.
- **A Steam Controller plugged back in is taken over again** by itself, and profiles apply as before. Pad angle is 0° by default.
- **A resting or landing thumb no longer wiggles the pointer or buzzes the haptics.** The pointer holds still while the finger rests on a trackpad and moves once it clearly does.
- **Trackpads move the Mac pointer by the exact amount** instead of rounding each step to a whole pixel.

## Graphics
- **Older 3D games can run on MoltenVK.** In Graphics, "Vulkan layer" switches Older 3D games from KosmicKrisp (DXVK 3) to MoltenVK (DXVK 1.10), the long-standing layer most Mac game tools use. Worth comparing per game; it isn't faster for every game.
- **New experimental fix: DirectX 8–9 through Metal (dgVoodoo2).** Hands a DirectX 8–9 game's graphics to dgVoodoo2, which draws through DirectX 11 — with the game set to Metal for DirectX 10–11 (DXMT), straight to Metal instead of through Vulkan. Downloads dgVoodoo2 2.87.6 (freeware), checked against its tested checksum; **Open dgVoodoo Settings…** under the fix opens its own settings; Undo removes it. Not yet working with 32-bit games such as Battlefield 2, which crash at start; for 64-bit DirectX 9 games it is untested.
